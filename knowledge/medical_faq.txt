## What is menstruation?
Menstruation, commonly referred to as a period, is the monthly shedding of the uterine lining (endometrium) in women of reproductive age. It occurs when an egg released during ovulation is not fertilized. The body sheds blood and tissue from inside the uterus through the cervix and vagina.

## Normal menstrual cycle length
A normal menstrual cycle typically ranges from 21 to 35 days, measured from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. The actual bleeding phase usually lasts between 2 to 7 days. Cycle lengths can vary slightly between individuals and throughout a person's reproductive life.

## What is PCOS?
Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting women of reproductive age. It is characterized by elevated levels of androgens (male hormones), irregular or absent menstrual cycles, and small fluid-filled sacs (follicles) on the ovaries observed via ultrasound.

## Symptoms of PCOS
Common symptoms of Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) include:
- Irregular, infrequent, or prolonged menstrual cycles
- Excess facial and body hair (hirsutism)
- Severe acne or oily skin
- Thinning hair on the scalp
- Weight gain or difficulty losing weight
- Darkening of the skin along neck creases and groin (acanthosis nigricans)

## Causes of menstrual cramps
Menstrual cramps (dysmenorrhea) are caused by contractions in the uterine muscle driven by hormone-like substances called prostaglandins. Higher levels of prostaglandins are associated with more severe cramping. Secondary causes may include underlying conditions like endometriosis, uterine fibroids, or adenomyosis.

## Heavy menstrual bleeding
Heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia) is characterized by period flow that interferes with physical, emotional, or social quality of life. Indicators include soaking through one or more pads/tampons every hour for consecutive hours, needing double sanitary protection, or passing blood clots larger than a quarter.

## Light menstrual bleeding
Light menstrual bleeding (hypomenorrhea) occurs when period flow is significantly less than average. Common causes include hormonal contraception (such as birth control pills or hormonal IUDs), stress, rapid weight changes, low body fat, perimenopause, or conditions affecting thyroid function.

## Estrogen
Estrogen is one of the primary female reproductive hormones. It regulates the growth of the uterine lining, supports ovulation, and contributes to bone, skin, and cardiovascular health. Abnormally low or high estrogen levels may contribute to irregular menstrual cycles, mood changes, and fertility issues.

## Progesterone
Progesterone is produced after ovulation and prepares the uterus for pregnancy. If pregnancy does not occur, progesterone levels fall, triggering menstruation. Low progesterone may contribute to irregular cycles or spotting.

## Hormonal Balance
The menstrual cycle is regulated by coordinated interactions among estrogen, progesterone, luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). Disturbances in these hormones may result in irregular periods, ovulation problems, infertility, or abnormal bleeding.

## Irregular periods
Irregular periods refer to menstrual cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days, longer than 35 days, or vary wildly in length month-to-month. Causes include hormonal imbalances (like PCOS or thyroid disorders), high stress, extreme exercise, significant weight changes, or approaching perimenopause.

## Ovulation
Ovulation is the phase in the menstrual cycle when a mature egg is released from the ovary into the fallopian tube, making it available for fertilization. It typically occurs around 12 to 16 days before the start of the next period.

## PMS
Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) refers to a combination of physical and emotional symptoms that occur in the one to two weeks leading up to a period. Symptoms may include mood swings, bloating, breast tenderness, fatigue, irritability, and food cravings, which usually resolve shortly after bleeding starts.

## Endometriosis
Endometriosis is a condition in which tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside the uterine cavity, commonly on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, and pelvic tissues. This displaced tissue thickens, breaks down, and bleeds with each menstrual cycle, leading to pain, inflammation, and potential scar tissue formation.

## Adenomyosis
Adenomyosis occurs when the tissue that normally lines the uterus (endometrium) grows into the muscular wall of the uterus (myometrium). The displaced tissue continues to act normally—thickening, breaking down, and bleeding during each cycle—resulting in an enlarged uterus, painful periods, and heavy bleeding.

## Stress and periods
High physical or emotional stress triggers the release of cortisol, which can disrupt the hypothalamus—the region of the brain that regulates hormones responsible for the menstrual cycle. This disruption can cause delayed periods, irregular cycles, or temporarily missed periods (amenorrhea).

## Sleep and hormones
Sleep quality directly influences the endocrine system. Poor or irregular sleep disrupts circadian rhythms, altering the secretion of reproductive hormones such as luteinizing hormone (LH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), and melatonin, which can lead to cycle irregularity and exacerbated PMS symptoms.

## Exercise during periods
Moderate physical activity during menstruation—such as walking, yoga, swimming, or light cardio—can help relieve period cramps and improve mood by releasing endorphins. It is advisable to listen to your body and adjust intensity levels based on fatigue or comfort.

## Diet during menstruation
Maintaining a balanced diet during menstruation helps manage symptoms and replenish lost nutrients. Focus on iron-rich foods, magnesium (to reduce cramps), complex carbohydrates (to stabilize blood sugar), and hydration. Limiting excess caffeine, alcohol, and high-sodium foods can reduce bloating and irritability.

## Iron deficiency
Menstrual blood loss is a leading cause of iron deficiency and iron-deficiency anemia in women of childbearing age. Symptoms include chronic fatigue, weakness, pale skin, cold hands and feet, and dizziness. Individuals with heavy periods should monitor their iron levels with a healthcare provider.

## When to consult a gynecologist
You should consult a gynecologist if you experience:
- Periods lasting longer than 8 days or soaking through protection every hour
- Severe pain that isn't relieved by over-the-counter medication
- Missed periods for 3 or more months (and you are not pregnant)
- Bleeding between periods or after sexual intercourse
- Cycles shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days

## Healthy Lifestyle for Menstrual Health
Maintaining menstrual health involves:
- Regular physical activity
- Balanced nutrition
- Adequate hydration
- Sleeping 7–9 hours per night
- Managing stress through relaxation techniques
- Maintaining a healthy body weight
- Attending routine gynecological check-ups

## Emergency Warning Signs
Seek immediate medical attention if any of the following occur:
- Sudden severe pelvic pain
- Fainting or loss of consciousness
- Heavy bleeding soaking through one pad every hour for several hours
- Fever with pelvic pain
- Severe abdominal pain after a missed period
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain
